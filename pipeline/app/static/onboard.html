Job Search — Setup

Checking your repository…

    Your resume

    Upload your resume as a DOCX, ODT, or PDF. DOCX/ODT are recommended — the AI tailors an editable resume per job far more cleanly than a PDF. We extract the text for keyword scoring and the cloud evaluator; it never leaves your machine except as an encrypted GitHub secret. We'll also auto-fill the About step from it.

    Free-text guidance the AI follows when tailoring your resume for each job — applied within the honesty rules (it can reorder and re-emphasize, never invent experience).

    About you

    Work authorization (optional — filters roles you can't take & answers screening questions)

    Country-neutral: describe your eligibility. A US citizen eyeing roles in the EU and an EU citizen eyeing the US fill this in the same way.

    Voluntary self-identification (optional — auto-answers EEO questions)

    Leave any field blank to decline ("prefer not to say") on every application — the default. Fill one in only to self-identify; it's matched against the form's options, so use the employer's wording.

    Some job sites ask you to consent to processing your self-identification data, and to save or share those answers, before an application can be submitted. You provide none either way, but the consent is required on the sites that ask for it.

    Search settings

    Put "Remote" in a chunk to route that pass through JobSpy's remote filter. Examples: Dallas, TX · London, UK.

    Job boards

    Career narrative (optional — the evaluator and your browser agent read this)

    Left blank, the headline and story are derived from your résumé's summary and your first target role — and stay blank here, so a new résumé re-derives them.

    AI evaluation provider

    Pick a provider and paste its API key. The key is written straight to a GitHub secret (via stdin) and never logged or stored locally.

    When on, evaluation and résumé tailoring stay within the model's RPM, TPM and RPD; work past the daily cap defers to the next run. On a low-TPM model (the Gemma rows) the token budget, not the request rate, is what sets the pace — so runs are slower than the RPM suggests.

    Google changes these, and they differ per tier — so the built-in numbers are only a fallback. Read your project's real values at aistudio.google.com/rate-limit and enter them here; they override the built-in table for this model, locally and in the cloud. Worth copying TPM too: on a low-TPM model it is the limit that actually paces a run, and a blank box leaves the built-in number in charge.

    Leave all three blank to keep using the built-in numbers.

    Daily digest (optional — after every cloud run, the roles worth your attention)

    One message per day naming the roles that scored at or above the minimum, with the evaluator's verdict, the posting link and the reports attached; a one-line notice when the run failed. Either channel is enough.

    Discord: Server Settings → Integrations → Webhooks → New Webhook → Copy Webhook URL — about two minutes, on any server you own (a private one is fine).

    Email instead / also

    Sent over STARTTLS. Gmail needs an App Password with 2-step verification on (Google Account → Security → App passwords); paste that password here, not your account password.

    Local settings (.env)

    Local settings saved to your .env — separate from the GitHub secrets above: the provider the UI uses for + Add Job evaluations and career skills, résumé-tailoring overrides, and your browser-agent handoff folder. You can skip this step and configure it later.
